Glasswing overwhelms maintainers with underwhelming findings (VulnCheck)
back

Patrick Garrity of VulnCheck has written a blog post that looks at vulnerabilities found by Anthropic's Project Glasswing since it launched on April 7, and finds it wanting:

Of the 37,966 findings Anthropic reports, only 7,349 (19.4%) have been made public. Just 217 of those have been marked fixed. The more glaring number is that 4.4% (1,654) of the project's findings have already been marked as duplicates, 7.5x more duplicates than fixes. On top of that, 419 findings have been withdrawn. It seems odd that the ledger now claims a 94.2% true positive rate, up from 90.8% at launch, when more findings have been withdrawn or marked as duplicates than fixed, likely a feedback loop missing to the triage rates listed as "True Positives".

[...] AI is a valuable tool for finding vulnerabilities, and I'm not discounting that. But seven months in, the evidence still doesn't match the launch narrative. The CVEs are a sliver of this year's total, almost none are being exploited, fixes have stalled while duplicates and withdrawals pile up, and the triage burden is shifting onto the open source maintainers Glasswing promised to help. The receipts keep coming in. They still don't reconcile.

Introducing Polo: a Wayland MATE compositor
back

Kerem Söke has announced the 1.0 release of Polo, a Wayland compositor for the MATE Desktop Environment:

The idea is to keep MATE behaving as closely as possible to how it does on X11, rather than changing the desktop to fit a different compositor. [...]

Window management, metacity, workspaces, tiling, XWayland, clipboard and clipman support, multiple monitors, display scaling, hotplugging, screenshots, screen sharing, and session management are all working.

I've also added desktop effects: fade, zoom, zap, spin, squeemize, magic lamp, desktop cube, expo, and wobbly windows.

Söke is looking for people to test Polo, particularly on multi-monitor setups, and report back with problems.

[$] C++26 contract confusion
back

The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) has blessed seven C++ language revisions since 1998. The language is mostly developed by ISO's Working Group 21, which considers proposed additions and amendments, integrates them into a document, and then presents that document to the main ISO body to be voted on as a standard. On October 23, for the first time ever, ISO declined to ratify a document presented by Working Group 21, leaving the future of the C++26 standard in doubt. The decision was driven by controversy around a proposed new "contracts" feature that would add pre- and post-conditions to function signatures.

Stenberg: 30 years of Internet transfers with curl
back
Daniel Stenberg reflects on the 30th anniversary of the curl project.

libcurl runs in an estimated thirty billion installations today. It is a rough estimate since the majority of all libcurl users never tell us they use it, but we need to find it mentioned in their credit screens, open source license windows or their manuals. A super popular app might use libcurl today and not tomorrow, or vice versa, which can make the numbers shift by several billions overnight. In reality of course, once the pulse raise has calmed down and your ego is content, these numbers do not matter. In our corner of the world, things don't change notably when we go from a thousand installs to billions. We still work with similar people, in similar ways, with similar challenges.

Zellij 0.46 released
back

Version 0.46 of the Zellij terminal workspace has been released.

This release includes a major addition in Zellij: Zellij is now also a desktop terminal. To run it: zellij terminal. It works on linux, mac and windows.

Nothing changes for the normal multiplexer - Zellij can still run inside another terminal. Both sessions are the same and can be shared.

[$] Additional hardening for the glibc dynamic linker
back

The Node.js project recently had a security vulnerability that affected its Brotli decoder. The bug was a buffer overflow, but the particular way that official Node.js binaries are built for Linux meant that one of the main protections against turning a buffer overflow into remote code execution, address space layout randomization (ASLR) could not be turned on. If ASLR had been enabled, the bug would still be exploitable in theory, but much more difficult to exploit in practice. In response, the GNU C Library (glibc) is considering a new set of tunable settings to allow administrators to forbid programs that do not support ASLR. The new settings, while a welcome hardening measure, may not address the root problem.

[$] Synaptics patent claims stop work on DisplayLink drivers
back

Mike Lothian owns a docking station (or dock) that uses a DisplayLink chipset that had, at least in his opinion, subpar performance on Linux. He decided to try improving the Linux driver, with LLM assistance, but ran into one problem that he could not solve—so he asked for help, hoping that Synaptics's developers would lend a hand. (DisplayLink was acquired by Synaptics in 2022.) Rather than help him in improving the driver, however, the Synaptics legal team responded with patent infringement claims, a demand that he cease unauthorized implementation of DisplayLink technology, and threats of expensive legal penalties. For now, it appears that Linux users of the company's docks will have to continue to live without feature parity with other platforms.
