Dave Täht given the Jonathan B. Postel Service Award
back
The Internet Society has announced that the Jonathan B. Postel Service Award has been awarded to Dave Täht for his many efforts to improve the Internet.

Täht's legacy can be found in community hubs in Nicaragua, where he worked to expand connectivity, and first discovered the urgency of solving bufferbloat. It can be found in every lag-free video call with a loved one. It can be found in outer space—Täht helped Starlink solve its latency challenges. And it can be found in the countless people he mentored and befriended over the years, who will not stop until bufferbloat stops getting in the way of band practice.

Dave is still deeply missed by those of us who knew him; this is an award he richly deserved.

[$] Tainting (or not) on manual driver binding
back
Early in the 7.4 merge window, Greg Kroah-Hartman applied a patch that caused the kernel to be marked as tainted whenever user space manually binds or unbinds a device driver to a device. This change was meant to be a defense against a flood of spurious bug reports generated by fuzzers, but it also turned out to get in the way of some real use cases. After some discussion, the patch has been reverted, but this situation hints at an area where better kernel interfaces are needed.

Beyls: Wrapping up LLVM's relicensing effort
back

Kristof Beyls has published a summary to the LLVM discussion forum to announce that the project is declaring success and bringing its relicensing effort to a close after eight years.

We've been asking past contributors to relicense their work since 2018. New contributions have come in under Apache-2.0 WITH LLVM-exception since January 19, 2019. Code contributed before that date was under LLVM's earlier licenses (UIUC/NCSA, plus MIT for some runtime libraries), so we needed permission from its authors to move it over. [...]

Today, more than 99.5% of all lines of code ever contributed to LLVM are either covered by the new license, too small for copyright to apply, or no longer in the repository.

Rather than trying to "chase the last tiny fraction forever" LLVM is winding up the effort. Any contributors with code contributed before January 19, 2019 who have not signed a relicensing agreement and do not want their contributions made available under Apache-2.0 with the LLVM-exception can still reach out to the project to ensure their code has the appropriate SPDX license identifier.

[$] Added indirection allows for larger BPF programs
back

The BPF verifier has historically worked by tracing every possible path of execution through a program, checking, at each instruction, that various safety properties hold. This works, but it can result in an exponential increase in the number of states that need checking in programs with complicated control-flow. Alexei Starovoitov, one of the kernel's core BPF maintainers, has shared a patch set that substantially reworks how the verifier tracks states, in order to reduce the size of that exponential explosion.
