Three studies link glyphosate to cancer, contradicting claims of Trump EPA
back

Experts say NIH research raises concerns about chemical companies’ influence on US agency’s pesticide division

The US government’s top medical research agency has released three new studies linking glyphosate, the world’s most widely used pesticide and an active ingredient in Roundup, to cancer. The National Institutes of Health (NIH) findings contradicts a controversial recent Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) conclusion that the weed killer is not a carcinogen, and deals a blow to the White House, which has been closely allied with Roundup producer Bayer.

The NIH’s three papers could have significant political and legal fallout. The EPA is in the process of a court-ordered re-evaluation of glyphosate’s cancer risks. The NIH findings generates new pressure for the EPA to find that the substance is linked to cancer, which would subject it to a modest increase in regulations.
