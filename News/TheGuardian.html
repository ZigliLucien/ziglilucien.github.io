Delaware hit by rare November tornado
back

Meanwhile, severe thunderstorms bring hailstones the size of baseballs in Argentina

The National Weather Service in the US has confirmed that an EF1 tornado touched down south-east of Newark in the Salem Woods neighbourhood, Delaware, on Thursday. The tornado reached 160 metres wide, had a path length of 4.4 miles (7.1km) and reached estimated peak winds of up to 100mph (161km/h). There were no injuries or fatalities but windows and roofs were damaged on several homes, a large hotel sign fell on to a number of cars, and a semi truck overturned on I95. Although Delaware often experiences a few tornadoes every year, this is only the second time the state has had tornado activity in November since 1950, according to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration. The only other November tornado recorded was in 1989.

Severe storms have also resulted in tornadoes in Argentina as well as large hailstones. A round of severe thunderstorms moved through the central Córdoba province on Friday, bringing hailstones up to the size of a baseball (about 7.5cm in diameter). Parts of the province recorded up to 73mm of rainfall in just six hours early on Saturday. Such storms are common at this time of year. Water vapour from the Amazon meeting the Andes quickly condenses, resulting in massive cloud formations often associated with heavy downpours, lightning, tornadoes and large hailstones.

Storm Charles causes floods and flight cancellations across southern Europe
back

Heavy rain and strong winds have also caused disruption across British Columbia, Canada, over the last week

Storm Charles has brought destructive weather to the Iberian peninsula this week, particularly in Portugal and the far south of Spain. Heavy rain and strong south-westerly winds arrived first in the Portuguese island of Madeira, before the system headed towards the mainland on 11 November. A wind gust of 84.5mph (136 km/h) was recorded at Funchal airport, alongside rainfall totals reaching 355mm (13.97in) in 12 hours. In Lisbon, 183 flights were cancelled on Wednesday, about a third of its normal daily traffic, while Madrid Barajas airport had 155 cancellations.

After causing widespread flooding in Spain and Portugal, the system moved on a north-easterly track through southern France and then towards northern Italy. This resulted in significant flooding in parts of Cannes, some of which caused rock falls and led to the closure of roads along the French Riviera. The Italian weather service issued a red rainfall warning, its highest level, for the area extending from Tuscany to Venezia. Further heavy rain is expected to fall in Italy and the Balkans over the next few days, with the latest forecast models indicating an additional 100mm could accumulate widely, while totals close to 200mm are likely in more mountainous areas.
