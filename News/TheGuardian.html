Storm Charles causes floods and flight cancellations across southern Europe
back

Heavy rain and strong winds have also caused disruption across British Columbia, Canada, over the last week

Storm Charles has brought destructive weather to the Iberian peninsula this week, particularly in Portugal and the far south of Spain. Heavy rain and strong south-westerly winds arrived first in the Portuguese island of Madeira, before the system headed towards the mainland on 11 November. A wind gust of 84.5mph (136 km/h) was recorded at Funchal airport, alongside rainfall totals reaching 355mm (13.97in) in 12 hours. In Lisbon, 183 flights were cancelled on Wednesday, about a third of its normal daily traffic, while Madrid Barajas airport had 155 cancellations.

After causing widespread flooding in Spain and Portugal, the system moved on a north-easterly track through southern France and then towards northern Italy. This resulted in significant flooding in parts of Cannes, some of which caused rock falls and led to the closure of roads along the French Riviera. The Italian weather service issued a red rainfall warning, its highest level, for the area extending from Tuscany to Venezia. Further heavy rain is expected to fall in Italy and the Balkans over the next few days, with the latest forecast models indicating an additional 100mm could accumulate widely, while totals close to 200mm are likely in more mountainous areas.

The Federal Aviation Administration declared a nationwide ground stop on Friday afternoon, throwing airports into sudden chaos, after a communications failure at air traffic control centres.

More than 6,600 flights were cancelled and 8,600 delayed, with another 1,500 already cancelled for Sunday, according to FlightRadar24. Aviation analysts said the four-hour nationwide grounding could disrupt airline ⁠schedules for days. It was the longest flight shutdown since the attacks of September 11 2001.

The FAA lifted the ground stop later on Friday, with transportation secretary Sean Duffy saying US airspace reopened at 8.15pm ET (0115 GMT). But departures would ⁠resume gradually and it would take ​time for airlines to restore normal operations, he warned. “It’s going to be slow.”

All US-bound departures from European airports are suspended until 5am UTC on Saturday, according to a notice published by the European traffic control agency, Eurocontrol.

US airlines including American, United and Southwest began resuming flights on Friday night. “Thank you for your understanding as we work as quickly as possible to fully restore normal operations,” Delta said.

Duffy said the federal government had “identified the problem” that brought down the US air traffic control system on Friday and it was “operational right now”. The system was “incredibly old”, he said. “Our routers actually got overloaded with data, and … they started to give us inconsistency in the data from those routers. And because of that, we shut the system down.”

There was no indication from preliminary investigations that the communications stoppage was tied to a “nefarious attack”, homeland security secretary Markwayne Mullin said.

Duffy blamed Congress for failing to fund a new air traffic control system that he said could prevent such outages, and called for $50bn to modernize it.
