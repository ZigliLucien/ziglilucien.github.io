Other Examples

Let us look at a few examples where Kravchuk polynomials appear quite naturally.

Random walk

Consider a one-dimensional random walk, ``drunkard's walk,'' of finite step size, call it 1 unit, on a grid of integers. A step is taken every second. After N seconds the drunkard, who has made a total of i steps forwards and j steps backwards (so N=i+j) is to be found at position x=i-j. Notice there are two sets of variables that are natural in the drunkard's world determining the space-time event: the usual coordinates of space (position) and time x,N, and isotropic variables i,j, with the change-of-coordinate rules:

x = i-j
N = i+j

and
i = 1
2		 (N+x)
j = 1
2		 (N-x)

An analogy with the space-time of relativity theory is clear: what is the maximum speed possible? where is the ``light cone''? Note that from the generating function it follows that the K-polynomials satisfy a discrete version of the wave equation in the variables i,j .

Urn model Or the mathematics of the I Ching

Suppose you have an urn that contains n balls of two sorts: blue and red. Suppose that every minute one ball changes its color (Feinsilver's Demon draws randomly a ball, repaints it, and locates back in the urn). The question is what states are stable in this process.

Comment: This is a Western paraphrase of the ancient Chinese divination/philosophical formal system of the I Ching where the current ``state of affairs'' is modeled by one of 64 hexagrams, consisting of six lines. A line may be whole or broken, representing Yang or Yin, respectively. The ``story of the Universe,'' or the development of real-life situations is proposed to consist of transformations of particular lines of the current hexagram corresponding to the Yin/Yang principles.
